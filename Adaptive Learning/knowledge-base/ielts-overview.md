# IELTS Academic: overview and task families

## Scope

This note is a short orientation to IELTS Academic based on official IELTS preparation pages. It describes the exam at a high level; it does not reproduce official sample passages, prompts, recordings, answer keys, or model responses. Do not use it to infer a candidate's band score.

## Four skills

IELTS assesses Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking. Academic and General Training share the Listening and Speaking tests, while their Reading and Writing tests differ. IELTS Academic Reading uses written passages and tasks that ask the candidate to locate, connect, or interpret information in the text.

## Listening task families

Official sample materials illustrate several task families: selecting one or more options, matching, labelling a plan/map/diagram, and completing forms, notes, tables, flow charts, sentences, or short answers. The candidate must use information from the recording; a practice item should not be answerable solely from outside knowledge.

## Reading task families

Official Academic sample materials include multiple choice, identifying information (True/False/Not Given), identifying a writer's views or claims (Yes/No/Not Given), matching headings or information, matching sentence endings, completion tasks, and short answers. The distinction between False and Not Given matters: False conflicts with the passage; Not Given is not established by it.

## Writing and Speaking

Academic Writing includes a task based on visual information such as a graph, table, chart, or diagram, and an essay responding to a point of view, argument, or problem. Speaking is an interactive conversation with an examiner and has three parts: questions about familiar personal topics, an individual long turn based on a topic, and a further discussion.

## Authoring guidance for practice content

- Keep each generated Reading question grounded in its supplied passage and make the evidence span identifiable.
- For True/False/Not Given, ensure the passage either supports the statement, contradicts it, or leaves it unresolved; avoid ambiguous mixtures.
- For Listening exercises, store the audio/transcript relationship and do not treat a transcript as a substitute for listening practice.
- Writing and Speaking prompts need an appropriate rubric if the product will evaluate responses. A prompt alone does not justify a band estimate.
- Label new content as original practice material, not an official IELTS question.
